# Key Findings

## 1. Tournament expansion

The modern World Cup expanded from 24 teams and 52 matches in 1994 to 48 teams and 104 matches in 2026.

## 2. Attendance growth was not driven by match count alone

The 2026 edition recorded approximately 7 million total attendance in the project dataset, but the stronger analytical signal is that average attendance also rose to roughly 65,000 while capacity utilisation reached 99.74%.

This means the record total cannot be explained only by the larger 104-match schedule.

## 3. Sporting output remained strong

The completed 2026 dataset contains 308 goals, equivalent to 2.96 goals per match. The sporting dashboard also evaluates draws, one-goal margins, larger winning margins and discipline rather than relying on total goals alone.

## 4. Commercial scale increased substantially

The 2026 financial model uses approximately $8.91bn in revenue/budget against approximately $3.76bn in World Cup cost, producing an analytical projected surplus of about $5.16bn.

These values are explicitly treated as budget/provisional rather than final audited World Cup actuals.

## 5. Digital engagement reached a different scale

The 2026 audience model contains approximately 5.2bn audience/engagement, 20bn digital/video views and 30bn social-media impressions in the report layer.

These are not presented as interchangeable unique-viewer counts.

## Final verdict

Across the dimensions that can be measured consistently—scale, stadium demand, sporting output, commercial potential and global engagement—the evidence does **not** support describing the 2026 World Cup as a poor tournament overall.

That conclusion is intentionally narrower than claiming every fan had a positive experience. Ticket affordability, travel burden, visa access and subjective supporter satisfaction require different evidence and remain analytical limitations rather than assumptions.
